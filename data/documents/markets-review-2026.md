# Crescent Ledger Bank: Markets Review 2026 (internal, fictional)

This review collects the research desk's views on the main markets our private-banking and
corporate clients hold. It is written for analysts and relationship managers. All companies,
figures and forecasts in it are fictional and used for training only.

## 1. The year in one paragraph

Markets spent the first half of the year waiting for interest rates to fall and the second
half reacting to oil. Share prices rose about 6% over the year, bonds returned about 4%, and
energy shares swung more than any other sector, ending the year 9% lower than they started.
Clients who held a broad mix of shares and bonds finished the year slightly ahead.

## 2. Interest rates and bonds

The central bank cut its policy rate twice, from 4.25% to 3.75%. Government bond prices rose
as rates fell, and the ten-year government bond yield ended the year at 3.9%. The desk expects
one or two further cuts next year, but warns that a surprise rise in inflation would reverse
the move quickly. Government bonds remain the desk's preferred safe holding for conservative
clients, and short-dated bonds are favoured over long-dated ones.

## 3. Energy

Oil traded in a wide range between 62 and 91 USD a barrel. Integrated oil companies such as
Gulf Petroleum kept paying steady dividends, but their share prices followed the oil price
closely. Solar and other renewable companies, including Sunrise Solar, grew their sales by
more than 20%, yet their share prices fell 15% as investors worried about financing costs.
The desk's severe-but-plausible stress scenario for the coming year is a 25% fall in energy
share prices, and a 40% fall is used as the extreme case.

## 4. Banks and financials

Regional banks benefited from higher interest income in the first half and from steady
deposit growth all year. Medina Bank grew its deposits by 7% and kept a conservative loan
book with few bad loans. As rates fall, the desk expects bank profits to flatten rather than
fall, because lending volumes should rise. Financials remain the largest sector in most of
our clients' share portfolios.

## 5. Concentration risk

The most common weakness the desk found in client portfolios this year was concentration:
too much of the portfolio in one sector or one company. A moderate client should keep any
single sector below 20% of the portfolio and any single company below 10%. Several corporate
clients hold more than 25% in one sector, usually the sector their own business works in.
Relationship managers are asked to raise this in every annual review meeting.

## 6. The desk's limits and stress tests

Every client portfolio is tested against sector shocks. The loss a client accepts from one
sector shock depends on the risk profile: 2% of the portfolio for conservative clients, 5% for
moderate clients, 8% for growth clients and 10% for aggressive clients. When a stress test
shows a loss above the limit, the analyst records it and the relationship manager discusses
options with the client: reducing the position, adding protection, or accepting the risk in
writing.

## 7. Outlook for next year

The desk's base case is modest growth with falling interest rates. Shares are expected to
return between 4% and 7%, bonds between 3% and 5%. The main risks are an oil price shock, a
return of inflation, and a sudden loss of confidence in regional banks. The desk recommends
broad diversification, short-dated government bonds as the safe holding, and a careful
review of any energy position above 20% of a moderate portfolio.
